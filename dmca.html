1. What this policy covers

This policy applies to material published on turbotutor.dpdns.org and to worksheets, notes and other materials the tutor provides to families. It sets out how notices of claimed copyright infringement are handled under the notice and takedown provisions of the Digital Millennium Copyright Act, 17 U.S.C. Section 512, and how a person whose material was removed can respond.

This site does not host user accounts, uploads, comments, forums, reviews or a file-sharing service. There is no content that visitors post. The material here is written, drawn, coded or licensed by the tutor, apart from the photographs and fonts described in section 9.

2. Before you send a notice

Copyright complaints are easier to resolve when the first message is specific. Please check these points first:

  • Confirm which page, file or worksheet contains the material, and give the full URL if there is one.
  • Check the photo credits page. Photographs of Formula 1 cars, drivers and circuits are used under the licences recorded there, which may be Creative Commons licences that allow this use with attribution.
  • Consider whether the use is permitted by fair use, by a quotation exception, or by a licence you or your organisation granted.
  • If the concern is about a student's own work, a lesson record or a privacy matter, the privacy policy explains how to ask for access, correction or deletion instead.
  • If you are a parent or guardian with a question about materials supplied to your family, a direct email usually resolves it faster than a formal notice.

3. What a valid notice must contain

To be actionable under Section 512, a notice should be written and include all of the following:

  1. A physical or electronic signature of the copyright owner, or of a person authorised to act for the owner.
  2. Identification of the copyrighted work claimed to have been infringed. If several works are covered by one notice, give a representative list.
  3. Identification of the material claimed to be infringing, with enough detail for it to be located, such as the exact URL, page title and the section of the page.
  4. Your name, mailing address, telephone number and email address, so the tutor can contact you.
  5. A statement that you have a good faith belief that the use of the material is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent or the law.
  6. A statement that the information in the notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are the copyright owner or are authorised to act on the owner's behalf.

A notice that omits these elements may not be enforceable, and may be returned with a request for the missing information. Please do not send payment card numbers, government identification or other sensitive data with a notice; none of it is needed.

Email divit.gangrade@gmail.com with the subject line "DMCA notice". A scanned signed notice attached as a PDF is acceptable. If you prefer to speak first, call or text +1 804 847 3236 during the hours above, but a formal notice must still be in writing.

A postal address in Richmond, Virginia is available on request for a notice that must be sent on paper. Ask by email and it will be provided to the person making the claim.

5. What happens after a notice

When a notice arrives, the tutor will:

  • acknowledge it, normally within 5 business days;
  • check that it meets the requirements in section 3 and that the material identified is actually on the site or in the materials;
  • remove or disable access to the identified material where the claim appears valid, and keep a record of what was removed and when;
  • tell the person who supplied the material, where that is someone other than the tutor, so they have a chance to respond;
  • tell you what was done, or explain why no action was taken.

Removal is not an admission that the material was infringing. It is a precaution taken while the claim is assessed. If a claim turns out to be mistaken, the material may be restored.

6. Counter-notification

If material you supplied was removed and you believe the removal was a mistake or a misidentification, you may send a counter-notification. It should be written and include:

  1. Your physical or electronic signature.
  2. Identification of the material that was removed, and the location where it appeared before removal.
  3. A statement under penalty of perjury that you have a good faith belief the material was removed as a result of mistake or misidentification.
  4. Your name, address and telephone number.
  5. A statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the federal district court for the judicial district in which your address is located, or, if your address is outside the United States, for a judicial district in which the operator may be found, and that you will accept service of process from the person who sent the original notice or that person's agent.

Send it to the same email address with the subject line "DMCA counter-notice". If it is complete, the original claimant will be sent a copy. Unless the claimant tells the tutor within 10 business days that a court action has been filed, the material may be restored. Nothing here requires the tutor to restore material that is unlawful, that infringes another right, or that the tutor is entitled to keep offline for another reason.

7. Repeat infringement

The tutor does not run a service with user accounts, so there is no account to terminate. If a family or student repeatedly supplies or shares material in breach of copyright, or repeatedly ignores a removal request, the tutor may stop providing materials to that family and may decline further tutoring. That is the practical equivalent of a repeat infringer policy here, and it is applied consistently.

8. If you think a notice was misused

Section 512(f) of the DMCA allows a person harmed by a knowingly false or materially misleading notice or counter-notice to recover damages, including costs and attorneys' fees. Please make sure a claim is accurate before sending it. If you are unsure whether material infringes, get advice first. Sending a notice in good faith after a reasonable check is fine; sending one to silence criticism or a competitor is not.

Those licences already permit the use made here. If you believe an attribution is incomplete or a licence condition is not being met, say so and it will be corrected promptly.

10. Designated agent

The operator has not registered a designated copyright agent with the United States Copyright Office, because this site does not host material uploaded by users and does not operate a service for which that registration is required. Notices sent to the address in section 4 are still received and handled as described in this policy. If the site ever begins hosting content supplied by visitors, a designated agent will be registered and this page will be updated before that feature goes live.

11. Changes to this policy

This policy may be updated when the site, the materials or the law change. The effective date at the top will change with it, and a material change will be described on this page. A notice already received is handled under the policy in force when it arrived.
